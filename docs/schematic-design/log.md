## 2026-08-08 (session 8) - swapped the ideal diode, and gave submodules a 5th pin
Started as "is the MAX40203 even available" and turned into three connected decisions. Full working in [power](power.md#ideal-diode---lm66100-u9) and [submodules](../design-choices/submodules.md#revision-5-pins-not-4---adding-a-senseid-line).

- **U9 MAX40203 → LM66100DCKT (C2832141).** Trigger was stock — **879 units from a single listing**. But the good reasons were elsewhere: **SC-70-6 instead of WLP-4 0.35mm chip-scale**, which deletes the "confirm the fab places 0.35mm WLCSP, it may cost more than the part does" question the section had been carrying; **$0.17 vs $0.77**; **110mΩ vs ~260mΩ**; and **IQ 150nA vs 300nA**, so it *beats* the incumbent on the exact criterion MAX40203 was picked over MAX40200 for.
- **Current was never the sizing driver, and I got that wrong first.** I argued BS+ couldn't feed submodules because the ideal diode is 1A — but **pre-PD, BS+ comes from Q1 (a ~4A FET) and U9 is reverse-blocking, carrying nothing.** U9 only ever carries the LDO's ~300mA. The whole "we need a bigger diode" branch was aimed at the wrong operating point.
- **Traced what the ideal diode is actually for**, because "OR-ing" undersells it. Without it, BS+ backfeeds `+5VA → L2 → SW → the TPS54302's high-side body diode → PD+`, putting **~4.3V on PD+ before PD has negotiated anything**. It's a rail-sequencing guard, not an efficiency part — a plain Schottky would block it too, and the near-zero drop turns out not to be load-bearing anywhere.
- **CE→VOUT is the trap.** I first said CE→GND; the datasheet's §8.3.2 says CE→VOUT for always-on reverse-current blocking. Getting it wrong doesn't disable the part — **it turns it into a plain switch with no blocking, and everything appears to work** while BS+ quietly feeds PD+. Worst possible failure shape, silent.
- **Added `BS+ SRC`** — the LM66100's open-drain ST pin, pulled to **+3V3 not VOUT** (VOUT is BS+ at 5V; RP2350 abs max is 3.6V). Gives a hardware readout of which source is feeding BS+. Went in as telemetry; turned out to be the safety interlock for the submodule power question.
- **Accepted the 5.95V risk deliberately, and proved it can't be fixed.** BS+max = the comparator's UTP = 5.95V against a 6.0V abs max. It's structurally unfixable: `LTP ≥ 5.68V` (vSafe5V + error budget) and `UTP > LTP`, so **UTP can never be below 5.5V**. A series element drops nothing in the state that matters (no current flows); a shunt clamp would need to discriminate at **50mV**, which no zener does. Only a >6V part closes it. Recorded as accepted, not open.
- **Submodule connector 4 → 5 pins: `ID GND 5V Rx Tx`.** The gap was never pin count — **Tx/Rx are already GPIO**, so a dumb module can just have them reconfigured. The gap was **detection**: the Rx-idle trick needs an MCU on the far end to drive Tx, so an MCU-less module reads as absent forever. One ADC pin with a divider fixes presence, identity *and* analog input.
- **Prepend, don't append.** `5V GND Rx Tx ID` is worse on two counts: it puts the analog pin next to Tx *and* moves 5V to an end position where a misaligned module can land it on a signal pin. Prepending keeps the existing four untouched. Corrected mid-decision too — I put ID next to 5V calling it "a quiet DC rail", and **it isn't**, it's the gated big buck sharing ripple with 30 LEDs.
- **The ID divider runs off +3V3, so it works with the corner rail switched off.** Firmware can read what's plugged into a corner *before* deciding to power it. Property worth designing around, not an accident.
- **Corner power: A2, one SY6288CAAC on the group, +5VP only.** Scored 399/510 against four alternatives. **The dual-source options don't survive any legal weighting** — for the shared-rail version to win, "works on a 5V-only source" would need a weight of **≈14.5 on a 10-point scale**, because A2 beats it on seven of eight criteria. **And submodules-only dual-sourcing is dominated** by the shared-rail version: same two switches, same reverse-blocking problem, a fraction of the benefit. That was the version I'd been drifting toward.
- **Then swapped the switch to AP2171WG-7 (C110466) — because of the *symbol*.** SY6288 doesn't exist in any KiCad library, **and I couldn't verify its pinout** (LCSC and Silergy both serve an HTML bot page instead of the PDF). Dropping a standard SOT-23-5 symbol onto a part whose pins might not match is the same silent-failure shape as CE→GND on the LM66100. AP2171W ships with KiCad, has **35,414 in stock** vs 17.6k, and costs $0.21. **The cost is real though: 1A instead of 2A, which is below the 4×300mA sum of the per-port allowances** — so the published contract becomes *"≤300mA per port AND ≤1A total"*. Arguably better, since a 1A group limit **enforces** the budget in hardware where the 2A part enforced nothing until you were 66% over — but it's a contract change, not a free swap. Bonus: AP2171W's **/FLG open-drain fault output** is free telemetry for one resistor and one of 6 spare pins.
- **Variant trap logged anyway, in case SY6288 comes back:** `SY6288A` is the same package, same 2A, one cent cheaper, and has **no protection at all**. The `C` suffix would be the entire reason for that part.
- **Why RGB-on-5V-only was rejected, and why the obvious reason is wrong.** riskable: *"what's the point if you can't blind someone with it?"* — but brightness is **already** clamped to ≤10/32 on SK9822 thermal grounds, so it'd have run at normal brightness with ~1A to spare on a 5V/3A source (Type-C advertises 3A through Rp with no PD at all). **The power was there; the rail architecture wasn't worth it.** Different statements, and only the second is true — worth recording so this doesn't get reopened on the first.
- **Found a 3-session-old budget error.** [pin-budget](../design-choices/pin-budget.md) has understated by **2** since session 4: its I²C row still says "2, shared by both PHYs" but session 4 moved to two separate buses. The table caught the extra INT and missed the extra bus. The log said **"budget now 40/48"** at the time, which only works with I²C = 4 — so log and table have disagreed for three sessions. Schematic was always right.
- **Budgets now: GPIO 42/48, ADC 6/8.** The four ADC channels are exactly the ones the current-sense cut freed — a deliberate re-spend, since they were guarding a fault the PD source already bounds and now enable a class of module that couldn't otherwise exist.
- **Reallocated the PIO/SM budget after finding a wrong assumption.** The whole "which sides get the hardware UARTs" decision rested on hardware being the *better* path. It isn't: the datasheet says the PL011 **"supports a maximum baud rate of UARTCLK/16 (7.8 Mbaud at 125MHz)"**, while a PIO UART at 8 cycles/bit does **~18.75 Mbaud**. So the slow ceiling was sitting on the inter-tile links — the ones carrying relayed traffic from every tile downstream — while a submodule knob got the fast one. **Swapped: all 4 inter-tile sides to PIO (8 SMs), 2 submodule corners to hardware UART (0 SMs), 2 corners to PIO (4).** Still **12/12** — it's a pure reallocation, not a trade. [working](../design-choices/comms.md#revisit-the-piosm-allocation-was-built-on-a-wrong-assumption)
- **What the PL011 is actually good for is CPU load, not speed** — zero SMs and a 32-byte FIFO. Which means it belongs on links that are low-rate and numerous, not high-rate and few. That's the sentence the original decision needed and didn't have.
- **Two things fell out free.** **F11 disappears from the entire design** — PIO functions are uniform across every GPIO, and the two hardware corners land on the only GPIO22–29 pairs that are TX/RX at F2 (**24/25 UART1, 28/29 UART0** — conveniently different UARTs; 22/23 and 26/27 are both UART1 CTS/RTS so could never have paired anyway). And **the 2+2 rotation pairing dissolves** — with all four sides on PIO they're identical, so there's no assignment to make and no opposing-axes constraint. That decision was right for its premise; the premise is gone and what replaces it is simpler.
- **Next budget to watch is DMA, not SMs** — 8 PIO inter-tile + 4 submodule directions + RGB SPI + ADC ≈ 16 against RP2350's 16 if everything is DMA'd. Low-rate links can be interrupt-driven.
- **Then found the corner-power decision was answering the wrong question, and redid it.** The requirement was always *"submodules work without PD"* — and I'd scored it as **one weighted criterion among eight**, produced a confident 399/510 winner that **fails the requirement**, and even ran a sensitivity analysis "proving" nothing could flip it. **A requirement is a gate, not a scoring row.** Options A and A2 fail the gate and were never candidates. Kept the wrong matrix in the doc with the lesson attached: *a weighted matrix can only compare options that already satisfy the requirements.*
- **It also inverted the "B is dominated by C" finding.** C only beat B by *also* delivering RGB-without-PD — which riskable and I had already decided against. Strip out a benefit you don't want and C's lead evaporates while its costs (touching the RGB rail, dragging the level shifter's VCCB, re-coupling RGB gating) remain. **B is right.**
- **New topology: `BS+ →U12(AP2171W)→ SM_BUS ←U15(LM66100)← +5VP`, then `SM_BUS →U16(AP2171W)→ SM+`.** No new BOM lines — LM66100 is already U9, AP2171W already the group switch.
- **The +5VP branch is a diode, not a switch, and that's load-bearing.** It has to block reverse current or BS+ backfeeds through the disabled big buck into PD+ — **the same L→SW→high-side-body-diode path U9 exists to block, one buck over.** An ideal diode does it with no GPIO, and the usual auto-OR ambiguity doesn't apply because **pre-PD +5VP is genuinely at 0V, not merely lower.**
- **U16 needs firmware control because U15 has no enable.** Post-PD the only other off-switch is the big buck, which takes RGB with it — reintroducing the exact coupling this was meant to remove.
- **Dropped 2 of 3 status pull-ups.** U12's /FLG is redundant (U12 and U16 are **in series at the same 1A**, so U16 sees the identical fault); U15's ST duplicates `BS+ SRC`. **Kept U16's /FLG** — because the obvious inference just broke: you'd normally read "silent UART = faulted", but **MCU-less modules are now supported and a silent UART is their normal state**, so /FLG is the only direct signal the rail tripped. An open-drain output nobody reads doesn't need a pull-up at all.
- **Generalised the E9 pull-down rule**, now that it's bitten twice: *anywhere a default-low state is load-bearing on an RP2350 pin, the pull-down must be ≤8.2kΩ; 4.7k is the safe pick.* 120µA × 100k clamps at 2.2V — above an AP2171W EN threshold, so a mis-configured pin could turn the submodule rail **on**.
- **One real bug in the drawn circuit:** U16's EN is tied to GND through a **0Ω** — AP2171W is active-high, so the submodule rail can never turn on as drawn. Needs 4.7kΩ + a `SM EN` GPIO.
- **I also called a second bug that wasn't one.** Claimed C114 had been "stolen" from U9, leaving it without a local input cap. **Wrong — U9's cap is C41**, repurposed from the 100µF BS+ bulk to 1µF on `+5VA`. Both LM66100s have their 1µF.
- **And that repurposing closed two older items at once.** (1) **Attach inrush is fixed** — [session 6](#2026-08-05-session-6---blind-datasheet-research-on-every-chip-then-wrote-the-calc-pages-out-properly) wanted C41 deleted because 110µF was presented to the connector against USB's 10µF ceiling; BS+ now carries **~2µF**. (2) The **capacitor footprint defect drops 5 → 4** — the 100µF-in-an-0402 is gone, leaving the 4× 22µF.
- **Found a doc/board mismatch while checking:** power.md claimed **C24 = 10µF**, *"exactly the pairing Torex characterises for the 3.00–3.50V row"*. The board has **1µF**, and has all along. Corrected in four places, including a handoff-dip figure that was **10× out** (31µs → 3.1µs — still harmless, the LDO has 1.6V of headroom). On a page whose whole premise is *"values here are as-built"*, that's exactly the drift it exists to catch.
- **GPIO 42 → 44/48** (`SM BS EN`, `SM EN`, `SM FLT` replace the single group enable). ADC unchanged at 6/8. 4 spare.
- **Unverified and flagged for pre-fab:** whether AP2171W's OCP latches or auto-retries. Diodes bot-blocks the datasheet the same way LCSC did for SY6288.
- **Next:** apply the U9 swap in KiCad (symbol/footprint/Value/LCSC move together — the BOM pulls Value), draw the submodule corners, then the two connector footprints.

## 2026-08-08 (session 7) - picked the edge connector, and it unblocked the per-side FET
Two decisions that turned out to be the same decision. Full working in [module-connectors](../design-choices/module-connectors.md#revisit-i-picked-a-real-connector-and-it-killed-the-custom-cutout-idea) and [power](power.md#hv-per-side-switches---picking-the-fet).

- **Bought a connector instead of designing one.** I'd gone deep on discrete pogo pins in routed board-edge cutouts with plated scallop cradles - 0mm gap so working height = cutout depth, `advance = cavity depth t` so conformity and protrusion are independent knobs, the whole tolerance stack. **The physics was fine; the risk wasn't.** 40 hand-soldered joints on an unproven custom footprint, a wear surface that's ENIG-on-a-depanelization-artifact rather than hard gold, and I'd already misread the same pin drawing twice in one sitting. Part is **PG-6P-2.5-5.5H-SM-RA** (Shenzhen Yiwei), 2 per side, one male + one female.
- **The gender-split trick got absorbed into the part.** "Split male/female down the middle" - the cleverest bit of the original design - is now just "put the male body on the left half, female on the right." Nothing to engineer.
- **The pinout grew 8 → 12 contacts:** `GND GND HV HV BS Tx | Rx BS HV HV GND GND`. Power went from 2× to **4× HV / 4× GND** because two 6P bodies hand you twelve contacts whether you want them or not. Kept **GND outboard** from the original sketch rather than reordering - the most exposed contacts should be ground, not a 20V rail.
- **The gender rule is rotational, not positional.** "Male on the left half" is meaningless once the edge rotates. The invariant is **clockwise around the perimeter, every edge is male-then-female** - and that's *why* it self-mates: two neighbouring tiles traverse their shared edge in opposite rotational senses, so male necessarily lands on female. Drawn J1/J3/J7/J5 male, J2/J4/J8/J6 female. Same trick as the palindrome, applied to gender instead of nets.
- **THE UNBLOCK: the per-side current budget finally exists.** 4 HV contacts × 1A = **4A hard ceiling per side**, design target **≥2A continuous**. That's the number the [FET research](../research/fet-switching-and-gate-drive.md) called *"the single biggest lever"* and refused to guess at - it's been blocking the per-side switch since session 6.
- **FET went AO3401A → AO4407A ×4.** Redoing the thermals honestly (200°C/W realistic edge copper, not the datasheet's 1in² reference board, and iterating the RDS(on) tempco) puts SOT-23 at **110°C at 40°C ambient** at 2A, and at the connector's 4A it doesn't converge at all - it thermally runs away. AO4407A is **28°C at 2A, 38°C at 4A**, costs **+$0.26/tile**, and adds **zero BOM lines** because Q2 is already that exact LCSC part (C2841482). Scored 392/460 vs 222 for the status quo. **The connector is now the current limit, not the switch** - which is the right way round, because it stops firmware OCP from being the only thing preventing a fire.
- **Side effect worth as much as the thermals:** AO4407A is ±25V Vgs. The research called AO3401A's ±12V against a 20V rail *"the single sharpest edge in this whole design"* - every position safe only because of its zener. **That failure mode is gone.** The 4× BZX84C10 go from mandatory to margin (keeping them anyway). The unresolved 38.7W soft-start SOA peak also gets a much bigger package to live in.
- **Where I checked myself:** I searched every 30V P-FET in SOT-23 on JLC before giving up on the package - AO3401A (47mΩ) is genuinely near the best and the only Basic part in the class. There was no drop-in upgrade; the package was the limit. Also re-ran the TPS1663 eFuse that lost the original cost decision - it scored **329, second place, 107 points above the status quo**. It was right about the things it was right about, it just costs 15× more than a part that gets most of the way there.
- **What the connector costs me:** no inset sequencing. Two molded bodies each carry HV, GND *and* signal, so all 12 contacts break together - hot-unplug safety now rests entirely on PD voltage. **Default 9V.** I'd first written 12V, but **12V isn't a required PD step** - the Power Rules give 9V/15V/20V above 5V and 12V is an optional extra a source may not offer. 9V is the better answer anyway: an arc needs **both** ~15V and ~0.4A for gold, and 9V misses on voltage regardless of downstream load. 15V is only arc-safe breaking ≤1 tile (0.33A), which is the case I don't care about. Costs power though - 18W/joint at 2A ≈ 3.6 tiles, so at 9V the array is **current-limited, not voltage-limited**, which is one more reason the FET needed current headroom. **Bench check:** session 2 says the TPS54302 can't make 5V below ~9V in, so the safest arc voltage is also the tightest buck input.
- **Carrying knowingly:** the part isn't in the LCSC/JLC catalogue at all (single-source, hand-soldered, buy spares), and **5.5mm is taller than the 4.00mm magnetic connector I partly rejected on bulk** - I'm not applying "low profile" evenly and said so in the doc. Needs checking against the real case cross-section.
- **Found while wiring it up: a MOSFET is a *unidirectional* switch.** The intrinsic body diode sits across source–drain and the gate has no control over it, so an open per-side switch blocks **outbound only** - a neighbour that enables its switch feeds current into me through my own body diode. The partition scheme still holds, but **cooperatively**: both sides of a boundary must be open, and then the two diodes oppose and the edge floats. What's not true is that a tile can unilaterally refuse. [design-choices/power](../design-choices/power.md#per-side-control) said "boundary switches stay open" as though that isolated both ways; corrected there and worked through in [power](power.md#the-body-diode---an-open-switch-only-blocks-outbound). **Not fixing it** - back-to-back FETs + a floating-source gate drive to defend against a firmware topology bug isn't worth $0.40/tile and the complexity.
- **Two things fall out of that.** (1) **Source goes on PD+, drain on the connector** - that orientation points every body diode inward, which is what makes the both-open case float. Flipped, every tile permanently powers its neighbours. (2) **Firmware must turn an edge switch off on neighbour loss**, or the next hotplug into that edge is a hard hot-insert with no soft-start anywhere in the path. That's what makes the Rx pull-down detection load-bearing rather than a nicety.
- **Settled the two datasheet unknowns** (the repo's RP2350 PDF was a **truncated download** - replaced). **RP2350-E9 is worse than "weak pull-down"**: it's an active ~120µA *source* that parks a floating input at **2.2V**, i.e. a phantom neighbour on every empty edge. Datasheet says 8.2kΩ or less overcomes it; against **VIL = 0.35×IOVDD = 1.155V** that's only 171mV of margin on a *typical* figure, so **fit 4.7kΩ** (591mV). 10k fails outright. Affects A2, fixed in A3.
- **And I was wrong about GPIO6/7.** I extrapolated the RP2040 `+2 = CTS` pattern and flagged the Bottom/Right pair as broken. **RP2350 adds an F11 column** that puts UART TX/RX back on the CTS/RTS pins, so GPIO6/7 *is* a valid UART1 pair - just **F11 where the other three sides are F2**. Real gotcha, but firmware's, not hardware's. Pin budget was right.
- **Wrote down why the pairing is 2+2 across opposing axes.** The *reason* was already recorded (firmware picks the important sides per config); what wasn't was which 2+2 delivers it. Left/Right on different UARTs and Top/Bottom on different UARTs means whichever axis is in use, **both** its sides get hardware UARTs. A 1+3 split has the same count and lands it on the wrong sides.
- **Killed the current sensing entirely.** Went to pick the sense amp and ended up questioning whether the sensing path earns its place. It doesn't: the **fast** fault (dead short) is bounded by the PD source's own limit, not by anything on my board - at the source's ~5A the AO4407A dissipates **0.33W** and isn't even warm. The **slow** fault (too many tiles on one joint) is preventable in firmware for free, because master already builds the tile map, so it can refuse to enable an over-budget path instead of tripping after the contacts have cooked. Re-scored in [design-choices/power](../design-choices/power.md#re-decision-does-this-need-per-edge-ocp-at-all): **383/450 for no sensing at all.**
- **The DNP hedge was dominated, and that's the lesson.** I'd proposed fitting the footprints unpopulated - then found **area is the binding constraint**, and a DNP pad costs exactly as much board as a populated one. It scored 181, below every real option. The hedge only ever made sense while I thought area was free.
- **And the eFuse beat the sense amp (307 vs 221).** TPS1663 consumes **zero ADC**, which turned out to matter more than its $6. The original discrete-vs-eFuse table scored **cost at weight 8 and never scored area or ADC at all** - and those are the two constraints that actually bind. **If this design needed per-edge OCP, the eFuse would now be the right answer, not the INA.** It doesn't, so it's moot - but the original decision has been right for a reason that stopped being the reason.
- **ADC budget 6/8 → 2/8**, GPIO42–45 freed. Giving up bring-up observability deliberately; a bench supply and a meter do the same job on a prototype and don't have to ship on every tile.
- **`INA180` is a trap worth recording:** cheapest in class and **71,901 in stock**, and SOT-23-**5** means no REF pin → unidirectional only, which the body-diode finding disqualifies outright. That finding earned its keep two days after I wrote it down.
- **Found a second bug: the research's gate-drive values are backwards.** `R_pu 39k` with `R_soft 470k` is a **divider**, giving `Vgs = −0.077 × PD+` → **−0.69V at 9V**. Drawn as specified the switch never turns on. R_pu has to be ~20× R_soft, not the reverse. Corrected to **R_pu 100k / R_soft 4.7k / C_soft 100nF / Rb 10k / Rbe 100k** → Vgs = −0.955×PD+, ~1ms ramp, ~0.5A inrush into 54µF. The research even computed zener current as `(20−10.6)/39k`, which assumes a gate that can reach the clamp - it can't with 470k in the path. The two halves of that note were never reconciled.
- **Cut the 4 gate clamps too.** At the 9V default Vgs is **−8.6V, 35%** of AO4407A's ±25V. They'd guard the flagged-only 20V mode for ~32mm² of board I don't have.
- **Accepted, not verified:** turn-off is structurally ~20× slower than turn-on (τ_off = R_pu×C = 10ms), because the on-ratio *requires* R_pu >> R_soft. Can't be tuned out, and a diode across R_pu would short it in the on-state. ~4.5W for ~11ms in the linear region, against SOP-8's transient impedance - survivable, and there's no fast-trip requirement left now that OCP is gone. 25mΩ shunt is fine as spec'd (0–100mV over 0–4A → gain-20 → 0–2.0V into the ADC), but nothing amplifies it and nothing ever has. Can't be dropped - firmware-OCP-via-ADC is the argument discrete won over the eFuse on.
- **Also pinned:** the level-shift NPN is **BC847B** (C20069135, Basic), no longer "BC847/MMBT2222 TBD". And BZX84C10 quantity in [chips](../chips.md) was **1×, should be 6×**.
- **Submodule connector closed: 4-pin machined (Swiss) pin headers**, socket on the tile. Asked riskable, who warned *"Swiss/machine pin headers are awesome. Though, they wear out. Dupont headers **never** wear out."* Real objection, wrong axis: submodules mount and stay, so the cycle count that kills a machined socket never accumulates - the opposite of the edge connectors where "people will reconfigure these a lot" is a stated constraint. Same project, opposite requirement, different answer. The bit the conversation missed is **which side gets the socket** - only the socket wears, so wear says put it on the cheap replaceable submodule, but that leaves four bare pins sticking out of every unoccupied corner. Took socket-on-tile: don't optimise against a thousand-cycle failure mode at the cost of something that's wrong every day. [working](../design-choices/submodules.md#picking-the-connector-body---machined-pin-headers)
- **Next:** draw the two connector footprints. Also still open from session 6: the cap respin (blocking), R30/R31 0.5% parts, TLV431→TLV431B.

## 2026-08-05 (session 6) - blind datasheet research on every chip, then wrote the calc pages out properly
Ran 11 independent agents, one per chip block, each given the project's power architecture and the role its part has to play but **deliberately not shown the schematic** - so their conclusions could be diffed against what i actually drew. Results in [research](../research/) (10 pages). No RP2350B page on purpose, i'm doing that one myself. Then filled in [power](power.md), [comms](comms.md), [keys](keys.md), [rgb](rgb.md) and [mcu](mcu.md) with as-built values plus the derivations.

- **THE BIG ONE - [capacitor footprints](implementation.md#capacitor-footprints).** 5 caps have values that don't exist in the 0402 footprint they're drawn in (4× 22µF, 1× 100µF), and C26/C31 are 10µF 0402 sitting on the 20V PD+ rail where only 6.3V parts exist. The "cap derating pass" that's been open since session 4 was the wrong framing - these parts can't be *ordered*, never mind derated.
- **C41 (100µF on BS+) should just be deleted.** Q1 is default-ON, so BS+ bulk is presented to the connector at attach: 110µF against USB's 10µF ceiling, ~550mA inrush vs a 500mA default source. Deleting it fixes the inrush *and* an unbuildable part. C24's 10µF is all the LDO asks for.
- **The LDO is the thermal bottleneck, and that makes hall bank-gating mandatory.** XC6220 dropping 5→3.3V at 400mA is TJ≈138°C at 25°C ambient, past its own 125°C design point. Safe ceiling is ~250mA. And **the GH39F's power-on settling time isn't specified anywhere**, which is exactly the number gating depends on - so that's a bench measurement before fab, not after.
- **Where the research was wrong and the board was right:** the comparator agent derived a 7.36V trip (more margin from vSafe5V than my 5.73V). But it only saw the LM2903 - it couldn't know BS+ gets dragged to the trip by Q1, and BS+ feeds an LDO with a **6.5V absolute max**. 7.36V would kill it. Good reminder that a per-chip read can't see a constraint living two parts away.
- **Where the board is tight:** falling trip is 5.68V against vSafe5V's 5.5V ceiling - 180mV, versus a ±157mV RSS error budget. Fix is a TLV431B (±0.5%) and 0.5% divider resistors, ~1 cent.
- **Smaller stuff found:** AM0:15/AM1:15 are floating (tie to GND); the USB SEL zener clamps at 5.1V but the mux VCC is 3.3V and its latch-up spec explicitly excludes the S pin (use a 3.3V zener); FUSB302 INT pull-ups are 100k vs 4.7k typ; no OVP on the FUSB302 VBUS sense pin; external ESD (TPD2E2U06) still not placed and the mux has no IEC 61000-4-2 rating at all.
- **Confirmed good:** per-port VBUS sensing wired correctly, Rd comes from the FUSB302's dead-battery clamp so no external 5.1k is needed (and checklist §6 is stale for saying otherwise), D±shorted at the receptacle per spec, mux VCC correctly on 3V3, scan budget ~116µs against 1ms even on the pessimistic slew-limited maths.
- **SK9822 brightness must be clamped to ≤10 of 32 in firmware** - the datasheet calls 11–32 "not recommended" on package thermal grounds. ~500mA chain at level 10 vs ~1.9A unrestricted. That's separate from the PD power budget.
- **3 datasheets in the folder are mislabeled** (SS54-schottky.pdf is actually a 1N5817; two "app notes" are unrelated ADJ issues). Agents fetched correct replacements plus 11 new datasheets.
- **Next:** the cap respin (blocking), tie off the spare mux channels, then the bank-gating FETs and the inter-tile/submodule connector sheets.

## 2026-08-05 (session 5) - RGB level shifter: read the datasheet, changed the part
Went to write up the level shifter as a "better safe than sorry" precaution and it turned out not to be one. Full working in [rgb](rgb.md#picking-the-level-shifter), snag writeup in [implementation](implementation.md#rgb-level-shifter).
- **it's mandatory, not cautious:** SK9822-EC20 **VIH min 3.4V**; the 3V3 rail's best case is 3.366V. direct drive is out of spec at *every* condition, not "marginal". my original "add 74AHCT if marginal" note was the wrong framing.
- **74LVC1G125 is a trap** - VIH = 0.7×VCC = **3.5V** at 5V, so it fails identically to direct drive. AHCT/HCT are the flat-2.0V-VIH families, LVC isn't. would absolutely have grabbed it for being the cheapest/best-stocked buffer on LCSC.
- **the real find:** the shifter is on **gated-5V** but the MCU is on **always-on 3V3**, so with the big buck off the MCU back-feeds a dead rail through the buffer's clamp diodes. same shape as all four cold-start latches - i just never ran that pass on the RGB sheet. lesson didn't transfer between sheets.
- **new part: SN74LVC2T45** (C15741, VSSOP-8, 17k stock, $0.16) - dual-supply VCCA=3V3/VCCB=+5VP, DIR high, **Ioff partial-power-down**. scored 323/370 vs 186 for the 74AHCT125 on the board. fallback 2× SN74LVC1T45 (C7843).
- **docs updated:** [rgb calcs](rgb.md) (math + full re-select), [chips](../chips.md) (level shifter had no BOM line at all), [checklist](../schematic-checklist.md) §5, [index](index.md) open items.
- **board is now behind the docs:** U8 74AHCT125 still placed on +5VP. swap is the next edit.
- **Next:** swap U8, then the cap-derating pass, then MCU/keys sheets.

## 2026-07-05 (session 2) - started drawing the front-end, hit a comparator snag

## 2026-07-05 - front-end topology settled (on paper)
- Worked out the **VBUS front-end** wiring: 2× SS54 combine ports → VIN; TLV1805 comparator watches VIN and drives Q1 (VBUS→BS+, default on) + Q2 (VBUS→PD+, default off) at ~6V.
- **Decided:** 3V3 LDO taps **BS+**, not the clean-buck output - otherwise chicken-and-egg (buck needs PD+ needs MCU needs 3V3). BS+ is live the instant any cable's 5V arrives.
- **Decided:** backfeed protection = **SS54 Schottky per port** on the VBUS→PD+ path (Schottky fine on HV rail).
- **Fixed BOM error:** LDO was listed `XC6220B332MR / 300mA / SOT-23-3` - real part is **XC6220B331MR, 1A, SOT-25 (5-pin, CE)**. CE ties to VIN (always-on).
- **Conventions locked:** net names PD+/BS+/gated-5V/3V3/GND; 0402 default for R/C/jumpers (exceptions: inductors, HV-rail bulk caps, SMA diodes); 0Ω/jumper on all digital enable/select pins (default populated).
